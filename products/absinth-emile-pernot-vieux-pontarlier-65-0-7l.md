## Beschreibung

Den Vieux Pontarlier Absinth gab es schon mal: Vor dem Absinth Verbot in Frankreich um 1900, war ein Absinth mit dem gleichen Namen eine der am meisten verkauften Sorten. Heute wird der **Vieux Pontarlier Absinth** aus der Emile Pernot Brennerei diesem Rezept nachempfunden, und auf die gleiche Art und Weise wie sein historischer Vorgänger hergestellt: Sorgfältige Auswahl der handverlesenen Kräuter und Pflanzen (im Vieux Pontarlier wird der regional wachsende große Wermut verwendet), eine schonende Destillation des Mazerats in über 100 Jahre alten Kupferbrennblasen. Der Vieux Pontarlier wird ohne Zugabe von Zucker oder Farbstoffen hergestellt und ist demnach ein richtiges Naturprodukt.

Seine smaragdgrüne Farbe gewinnt der Vieux Pontarlier Absinth durch den natürlichen Färbungsprozess nach der Destillation. Die Farbe ändert sich bei der Zugabe von Wasser – der Absinth wird langsam trüb und das Ergebnis ist ein hellgrünes, beinahe gelbliches Getränk.

Der Geschmack des Vieux Pontarlier Absinths ist frisch mit viel grünem Anis, Wermut und leicht minzige Noten sorgen für ein rundum erfrischendes Erlebnis.

### ★★★★★ Zach · 2021-08-01

I have only been drinking absinthe for less than a year. At this point I have tried around 15 different absinthes. Vieux Pontarlier was the second absinthe I experienced, and it has remained the favorite by a wide margin. Not only is it the absinthe I compare all other absinthes too, it has become the alcohol that I compare all other alcohols.

A short story: Only a month or two before discovering Vieux Pontarlier I had discovered my favorite rum of all time - I even drove all over the area to different liquor vendors eventually purchasing 3 bottles of this rum and another related bottle (and I wasn't the only one buying it up, I discovered, someone else in the area was collecting this rum as well making it harder to find in my area). As a creative person absinthe intrigued me and after much research I highlighted Vieux Pontarlier as one that I would like to track down and try. It was unavailable in my area but during a road trip I was able to find a bottle and purchase it. Now that I have tasted Vieux my favorite rum is absolutely undrinkable. Buying one bottle of Vieux Pontarlier rendered 3 bottles of my previous favorite alcohol trash that I'm trying to pawn off on others at parties. Generally, I like to keep a few things hanging around for the sake of variety. Vieux Pontarlier was so next level, it seems a shame and a disappointment whenever I drink something else, even when I'm with other liquor aficionados. They bring their best whiskey/bourbon, scotch or whatever else for me to try and I always think to myself, "I'd rather drink Vieux Pontarlier" every time.

I will note: To date, the majority of absinthes I've tried have not been all that special, and a good number of them I do not like at all (even some highly-esteemed ones). Also, I have discovered that I do not prefer to drink absinthe in the traditional method - I drink extremely tiny sips, with very little to no dillution, no sugar, at room temperature. This coincidentally happens to be a recommended method for bringing out some of the flavors that are associated with Vieux Pontarlier. It is impossible to pour an amount small enough for the amount that I sip, and I have some speciality containers that allow me to drink this absinthe without the trouble of trying to pour so little into a glass.

The only absinthe that can even stand near Vieux Pontarlier, in my opinion, is the Jade 1901. I can't imagine someone liking one and not the other. But Vieux Pontarlier is my number one, and Jade 1901 (although a fantastic absinthe in it's own right) is still just a second. I haven't found a 3rd favorite yet although I have also enjoyed the 1789 Roquette, Lucid, and the Combier Blanchette.

Hopefully this gives you some insight into this absinthe and some context for my experience to make an informed decision and purchase.

### ★★★★☆ Emerald · 2014-05-06

Unlouched the colour is a brilliant peridot green, easily equaling the 'pair 'o' doe' eyes effect people talk about when first pouring a glass of the Jade PF 1901. The louche itself is quick to develop, starting with swirling oil patterns that quickly build to spiralling pale clouds, with a clear demarcation of colour as the louche gradually builds from top to bottom. This is a louche to rival that of the famous Jade range, no coaxing needed, no fancy tricks or timing, just drip water and watch the magic unfold - perfect. Colour after louche is a thick creamy looking pale yellowish green.

Taste wise this would be an excellent Absinthe for a first timer who might not be sure about the typical Anise/Licorice based taste of most other Absinthes. Two of the holy trinity take a back seat to the Wormwood, with the Anise and Fennel only coming through later as an after thought. This is a very Wormwood forward absinthe, and it takes a while for the palate to adjust before the full range of herbal flavours can be appreciated. This is the sort of absinthe it's hard to judge until you've more or less drunk your first full glass. It is extremely smooth and well blended, a little too well blended first impressions will tell you, but be patient and you can tease out subtle variations of flavour. There's the earthy spiciness of Wormwood that comes through first, followed by a minty warmth that coats and numbs the palate slightly - again showing this is a Wormwood forward Absinthe. The Anise and Fennel are there in the background, the last to arrive and barely noticeable until the last few sips of the drink when they start to make their presence a little more known. This isn't so much an absinthe you drink, as one you discover.

Imagine the roaring 20's, a smoky Jazz bar in downtown New Orleans, a few select bottles of bootleg absinthe under the counter for those in the know. If the Jade PF 1901 is the band leader up there in the spot light, then the Vieux Pontarlier is the pin curled blonde sitting in the booths waiting to be discovered.
